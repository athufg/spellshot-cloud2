## I. The Sundering

It is recorded, in every account that survives, that there was once a single current beneath the world: the Ley, unbroken and entire, from which all elemental force descended before it had cause to divide. No fire existed apart from ice, no lightning apart from stone. There was only the Ley, whole, and the world above it, ignorant of what it rested upon.

Yet "whole" was never the same as "without feature." Every account agrees the Ley held two faces even in its unity, perfectly joined and perfectly opposed — the dark and the pale, the still and the active, the hidden and the revealed. The eastern chronicles give these faces names older than the chronicles themselves: Yin, for the dark face, and Yang, for the pale. Before the Sundering, Yin and Yang were not two things but one — a single current with two ways of catching the light, the way a single coin has two faces without being two coins.

What ended this unity is disputed. The eastern chronicles name it a war between powers no longer remembered; the western name it a theft; the priesthoods of the Hub name it a correction visited upon the world by the Ley itself, for reasons it did not trouble to explain. The event itself, whatever its cause, is called by all the same name: the Sundering.

In that instant, the Ley broke — cleanly, into four complete and self-sufficient fragments, each carrying the Ley's elemental substance whole and entire unto itself: **Pyro, Zephyr, Aqua, and Terra.** These four, and no others, are Cores in the full sense the word carries today — a nature a Duelist may bind to once, completely, and forever.

Yin and Yang parted in that same instant, for the first and, by every surviving account, the only time since the world began. But a face is not a substance, and what held together for Pyro or Terra did not hold for them. Each flared apart into its own likeness for the space of a single unstable moment — long enough to scatter a handful of fragments outward, as the four classical Cores did in their own parting — and then, being two halves of one thing rather than two things in truth, neither could stand alone. Both dissolved, drawn back toward the reunion their nature demanded, and slipped out of the world's grasp entirely. **No Core of pure Yin or pure Yang exists in the world today.** Most who still tell the old stories believe there never will be one again. The chronicles are not so certain, and say only that what was never truly two things to begin with has not, perhaps, finished becoming one again — a matter this codex leaves where it finds it, for a telling the world has not yet earned.

Around all four classical Cores — and from Yin and Yang's single flare — lesser fragments were cast outward in the Sundering's violence: pieces too small and too incomplete to stand as a Core in their own right. These are the Echoes, and they number, by even the most conservative reckoning, in the thousands.

**Two things carried today are shaped from Yin and Yang's one flare, and are Echoes, not Cores.** Where a Duelist has gathered enough of Yang's scattered likeness to shape it into a working whole — a basic strike, a signature ability, a quiet advantage, carried together the way a Core's gifts are carried — the world calls it **Radiant**. Where the same is done with Yin's likeness, the world calls it **Umbra**. Both are real, both are whole enough to fight with, and both are spoken of as if they were Elements in their own right, because in every practical sense they are. But neither is a Bond. A Duelist who carries Radiant has not been chosen by Yang the way a Pyro Duelist has been chosen by fire; they have gathered and shaped a fragment of it, as diligently as any Duelist gathers any Echo, and may set it down again exactly as freely.

**Cryo, Voltaic, and Toxin are Echoes of a plainer kind — the four classical Cores meeting each other, nothing more.** Where Echoes of two classical Cores have been found, gathered, and shaped together often enough to be carried as a working whole rather than a single borrowed function, the world has given the result its own name. None of the three carries any trace of Yin or Yang; whatever the exact meeting that shapes each one, it is a meeting of classical substance alone.

Four Cores, then, are known and bound in the world today — no more, no fewer. Five Elements beside them are carried as whole, working things, gathered from Echoes rather than given by a Bond: Radiant, Umbra, Cryo, Voltaic, Toxin. The distinction between the two kinds is not a matter of degree. It is the whole of this codex's purpose to explain.

## II. The Bond

A Core does not instruct. It does not apprentice a supplicant over years, nor grant its power by degrees. A Core chooses, and having chosen, it binds itself to the one it has chosen entirely and at once. This binding is called Attunement, and it is permanent. It is also rare — most Duelists who ever take up arms will never be chosen by a Core at all, and will fight their whole lives on gathered Echoes alone, which is no less honorable a path and very much the more common one.

What passes to the Duelist in Attunement is not a set of techniques to be learned separately, for the Core does not conceive of itself in parts. A Core is a single temperament of power, and what a Duelist receives — the manner of their basic strike, the shape of their signature ability, the quiet advantage that follows them without their willing it — are not three gifts but three expressions of one nature, no more separable from each other than a person's gait, their voice, and their bearing might be separated and distributed to different owners. To take Pyro's strike without Pyro's temperament would not be to take a smaller gift; it would be to take nothing coherent at all.

For this reason, no Duelist has ever divided a Core, and no Duelist has ever held more than one of the four. It is not forbidden; it has simply never been achieved, for a Core does not share the space of its binding, and a Duelist's capacity for Attunement, whatever its true nature, has never been observed to exceed one bond at a time. A Duelist who has been Attuned is known by it for the rest of their life, and by every account, never once sets it aside for another.

## III. The Echoes

The Echoes are of a different order entirely. Too fragmentary to bind wholly with anyone, they may nonetheless be found, studied, and carried — and unlike a Core, an Echo does not demand exclusivity. Any Duelist, regardless of whether they carry a Core at all, may learn to carry an Echo, for what passes between an Echo and its bearer is not a binding of nature but a loan of function: a manner of movement, a means of defense, a singular and decisive act reserved for the moment it is needed most.

It follows, then, that a Duelist Attuned to Terra may carry an Echo of Aqua's making, and another of Zephyr's, without contradiction — for they have not divided their loyalty to their Core; they have merely gathered, as any capable Duelist must, the loose inheritance the Sundering scattered across the world for anyone diligent enough to claim it.

**Some Echoes run deeper than a single borrowed function.** Where enough related Echoes have been gathered and practiced together, a Duelist may shape them into a working whole indistinguishable, in the moment of a Duel, from a true Core's gift — a basic strike, a signature ability, a quiet advantage, all their own. Radiant, Umbra, Cryo, Voltaic, and Toxin are each carried this way. The difference from a true Core is never visible in the fight itself. It is visible only in what it costs to set down: a Duelist may abandon an Echo-shaped Element entirely and take up another — or take up a true Core instead, should one ever choose them — without consequence, the way they might set down any Echo. A Duelist who has been Attuned to one of the four has never been observed to do the same, not once, in the whole of the record.

## IV. The Duelist

A Duelist is neither chosen by prophecy nor risen through inheritance. Most are never chosen at all — they gather Echoes, shape them into whatever Element suits them, and fight on that gathered strength for the whole of their career, free to set it aside for another whenever they wish. The few a Core does choose, it chooses without explanation, indifferent to lineage, station, or merit foreseen: one who was ordinary the day before wakes bound to a Core the day after, and is never free of it again.

What a Duelist does with either kind of strength is a matter of standing among their own kind alone. Duels are not fought in war, nor for territory, nor for any purpose beyond the proving of a Bond — or a gathered mastery — against another's: a custom closer to trial than to conflict, by which a Duelist's command of their Element, true Core or shaped Echo alike, may be measured and made known. It is through this repeated proving, accumulated over seasons, that a Duelist may in time be named Ascendant — a rank at which the wider world ceases to ask what a Duelist carries, and asks instead only whether they are among the few who have proven themselves beyond question.

## V. The Unfound Cores

It is a matter of settled record that four Cores are known and bound in the world today. It does not follow that four is the whole and final number the world will ever know.

In the ages since the Sundering, scattered Echoes of the four classical Cores — cast far from their origin and left long undisturbed — have in certain places drifted together. Where an Echo of one Core's making has come to rest for centuries beside an Echo of another's, under conditions no scholar has yet fully accounted for, the two have on rare occasion ceased to behave as loose fragments altogether, and fused. What results is not a copy of either parent Core, nor a mere combination of their traits, but something new entire — a fifth true Core, complete and self-sufficient exactly as the four already known are, yet born of two natures rather than one, formed over centuries rather than in the Sundering's own instant.

These fused Cores are called the Unfound, not because they do not exist, but because they have not yet been located, and because — until a Duelist first Attunes to one and Attunement makes its nature known — the world cannot say with certainty what any given Unfound Core will be. What can be said with certainty is only this: an Unfound Core, wherever and whenever it is discovered, will carry the inherited character of two of the four classical Cores, expressed as something the world has not previously seen. It is by this same principle that every Unfound Core, once bound, is understood to seed further Echoes of its own — for having become whole, it sunders its own excess outward precisely as the first four once did, and the world's stock of loose fragments grows by exactly as much as its stock of Cores does.

This is a separate matter entirely from Yin and Yang's old flare, and the codex does not conflate them. An Unfound Core is a new thing, made of what the four classical Cores left behind. What Yin and Yang might yet become, should they ever hold together, would not be found this way, and would not be a Core like any other — it is for this reason the chronicles speak of it, when they speak of it at all, as a return rather than a discovery.

It is for this reason the scholars of the Hub maintain, against those who insist the world's magic is a finite and dwindling inheritance, that the Ley has not stopped becoming. It has only slowed.

- **Resolved, 2026-10-29 (supersedes the 2026-10-13 revision's "nine Cores, all full peers" structure):** only Pyro, Zephyr, Aqua, Terra are true Cores. Radiant and Umbra are Echoes shaped from Yin and Yang's single flare at the Sundering, not Yin/Yang "unmixed" — they carry no Bond and are fully swappable. Cryo, Voltaic, and Toxin have **no relation to Yin or Yang at all** — the old "compound Core" framing (a classical Core fused with a polarity) is retired outright, with no replacement pairing locked. They are Echoes of the four classical Cores meeting each other; this codex deliberately does not specify an exact recipe for any of the three, so as not to manufacture a new unconfirmed canon fact nobody asked for. P18 on the Problem Sheet (the old pairing question) is moot under this structure, not answered — see below.
- **Resolved, 2026-10-29 (closes P19):** the Hub's Ley Crystal being currently whole is Yin and Yang's current absence, made physical — not a plot hole against the Sundering being ancient history. Its eventual cracking, whenever the story gets there, is their literal return: the first time they will ever have held together since the Sundering, not a repeat of it. The earlier "fresh echo happening now" reading used to answer P2 is superseded by this — cleaner and now owner-confirmed.
- **New gameplay fact, owner-confirmed 2026-10-29, not yet in the Design Manual:** every non-classical Element (Radiant, Umbra, Cryo, Voltaic, Toxin, and anything added before Yin/Yang launch) stays freely swappable between matches, same as live today — being an Echo, not a Core, is exactly why. **When Yin and Yang eventually launch as playable Elements, far down the roadmap, they are the one deliberate exception:** a player chooses one or the other, and once chosen, is locked out of the converse permanently — no switching back, ever. This is the one place in the whole game where the lore's "a Core binds once, completely, forever" becomes an enforced mechanic instead of flavor text. Not yet decided: whether Radiant/Umbra get retired, folded into, or left standing alongside the real Yin/Yang Cores once they launch (they'd become narratively redundant — an echo of Yang sitting next to the real thing) — flagged for whoever picks this up when Yin/Yang actually nears development, not an open question to chase now.
- **Known, deliberately unresolved asymmetry:** the four classical Cores are described in-fiction as equally permanent/exclusive as Yin/Yang will be, but nothing in live gameplay currently stops a player from freely swapping Pyro↔Terra the way they swap any Echo Element. Only Yin/Yang get an enforced permanent-choice mechanic. This gap (lore-permanent-but-mechanically-free for the four classical Cores) is intentional and known, not an oversight — noted here so a future session doesn't "fix" it as a bug.

None of these are the lore concepts described in this file. Worth a naming pass eventually so player-facing copy doesn't collide the two meanings; not urgent, not yet discussed with the owner.
