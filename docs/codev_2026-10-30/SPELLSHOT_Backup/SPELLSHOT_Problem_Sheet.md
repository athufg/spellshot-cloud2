## Ability & Movement Balance

**P23 — Full ability + movement balance audit: why do players just rush without any strategic approach, the way they wouldn't in Marvel Rivals?**
Status: OPEN. Owner commissioned a complete Opus 5.5 pass (2026-10-16) over every ability on all 9 built Elements (Aqua, Cryo, Pyro, Radiant, Terra, Toxin, Umbra, Voltaic, Zephyr) plus shared Techniques, and the whole movement kit (run, slide, slide-jump, double jump, wall-run, wall-jump, every Element's own dash/leap/teleport) — explicitly scoped to include movement, not just spells, per the owner. Sourced from live Studio code (not IMPLEMENTED.md, which is a cross-check only, given its own track record of going stale — see P6/P13/P14). Four simulation passes requested, priority-ordered: TTK matrix (all 81 Element pairings), rush-vs-kite win rates (the one aimed directly at the named problem — reports the mechanical cause when rushing wins, e.g. gap-close speed outpacing available slows/roots, or melee out-damaging ranged per mana), mana-economy/sustained-DPS (catches spam-favored abilities as a separate cause of "no strategy"), and a gap-closer-vs-kiting-tool audit. P11's unconfirmed Cryo numbers (Deep Freeze root duration, Blizzard radius/duration, Mirror Cage volley length) were folded in as specific asks rather than left for a separate pass. Explicitly read-only — the prompt forbids editing any live value; this is report-only, same "stop before touching anything further" shape as the map build prompt's colour gate. Prompt sent via `Opus55_SpellshotBalanceAudit_Prompt.md`.

**RESULTS IN (2026-10-16, analysed 2026-10-17), delivered as the `SPELLSHOT Balance Sheet` dashboard artifact.** Status: OPEN — results in, no changes made yet, owner to pick from the 15 numbered recommendations. Headline findings, ranked by how much they actually explain the "people just rush" feel:
1. **Pyro is the bigger problem, bigger than rush-down itself.** Its mean TTK across all 9 matchups is ~3.0–5.4 s; every other Element sits at 5–13 s. It's simultaneously the roster's best rusher (wins ≥65% rushing 7 of 8 opponents) and best kiter (stops every rusher except Terra). This isn't a rush-vs-kite problem, it's a flat power problem independent of playstyle.
2. **Aqua and Toxin — the two kits built for a patient/ranged game — lose to being rushed by literally everyone (85–100% of the time, every Element), and never win by rushing anyone themselves.** This is the actual mechanism behind "no strategy": pick the kit built for ranged/DoT play and you get closed down and killed regardless of who's doing it. Rushing isn't universally dominant in the matrix (only 37/81 pairings favor the rusher), but it's dominant against the specific Elements meant to reward patience, which is enough to teach the whole playerbase the lesson the owner described.
3. **Cryo is the only kit whose own tools fight the gap-close race itself** (Blizzard and 2+ Chill stacks drop a chaser's speed under both the dash and wall-run thresholds) — contact time balloons 6–9× (e.g. 30.3 s instead of ~3.9 s) in matchups where Cryo kites. It still loses the fight once contact happens against the roster's top 4 kits, which is the live evidence behind recommendation #3 (make slows reliably clear those thresholds, not just Cryo's).
4. **Most Element dashes are pointless for closing distance** — the shared base movement chain alone (slide → slide-jump → double jump, repeated) sustains ~59 studs/s, faster than Bulwark Charge (42), Skyrider (42–55) and Cross-Slash (15–27). Only Shadow Step's blink (~183 studs/s) and Overcharge's burst (69 studs/s) actually beat just running.
5. **Several ability slots are dead weight in a 1v1 duel specifically**: Ember Refund and Chain Reaction only trigger on a kill (the round is already over), Antitoxin Vent cleanses a poison a non-Toxin opponent can't give you, Overcharge's prime needs a second enemy to chain to. Its own slot in the roster, doing nothing — a separate source of "no decisions to make" from rushing.
6. Also caught: real data bugs independent of any balance opinion (Boulder Shot's full charge travels slower than no charge; Fireball's range field vs. its actual 162-stud flight disagree; Undertow's two range fields disagree; a stale `AbilityHandler` constant; an unlogged live edit to Chain Bolt's range; a stray leftover `DashDistance` on Zephyr) — recommendation #15, high confidence, no judgment call needed.

Full stats tables, the 81-pairing matrix, and all 15 numbered recommendations (each with a target value and confidence rating) are in the artifact itself.

**CHANGES APPLIED IN STUDIO (2026-10-17), re-simulated, analysed same day.** Status: OPEN — partial fix, not closed. The artifact gained a `pairings_before`/`pairings_after` comparison (same 60-duels-a-pairing model, re-baselined) plus `shared_v2` (sustained chain 59 → an *estimated* 45 studs/s — not re-measured in Play, flagged by the audit itself). Verdict on each finding above:
- **Pyro (finding 1): barely moved, still the core problem.** Mean TTK 3.56 s → 3.87 s — recommendation #6 aimed for ~5 s and got about a third of the way there. Pyro is still ~1 s faster to kill than the next-fastest Element and about half the roster's median. Worse: Pyro's win rate rushing Cryo went *up* (88% → 98%) even though Cryo's slows now make Pyro take 48.6 s to reach it instead of 28 s — proof the problem is Pyro's power level, not contact time, and slowing the chase doesn't fix it on its own.
- **Toxin (part of finding 2): this one actually worked.** Its rush_win-against-it dropped hard (vs. Cryo 100%→67%, vs. Terra 100%→88%, vs. Zephyr 92%→63%) — the DoT buff (#7) made it a real kiter, not just a faster loss. But it had a side effect nobody asked for: Toxin rushing Aqua jumped from a 12% win rate to 92% — the same buff that fixed its kiting made it scary as a rusher too, specifically against Aqua.
- **Aqua (the other half of finding 2): didn't work.** It still gets rushed down by everyone at the same 87–100% rate as before the pass, matchup for matchup. Its average TTK dropped (9.06 s → 7.11 s), but that's a *faster loss*, not a fix — don't read that number as progress.
- **Cryo's stalling game (finding 3): confirmed and amplified**, as intended — contact time against it roughly doubled across the board (e.g. Pyro 28→48.6 s, Voltaic 18→28.2 s) once the chain was slowed (#2) and slows were made to actually clear the dash threshold (#3).
- **Two unflagged side effects worth a second look before calling this done:** Voltaic's mean TTK dropped 5.9 s → 4.8 s and it now sometimes wins by rushing when it almost never did — not one of the 15 recommendations named Voltaic directly, so this is likely a knock-on effect of #10's kit-piece rework (Overcharge's prime) and worth confirming it was intended. Zephyr's win rate rushing Cryo jumped 3%→52%, consistent with the Skyrider speed buff (#9) working harder than expected in that one matchup.
- **Net rush-vs-kite shift: small.** Cells favoring the rusher moved from 37/81 to 36/81 — the pass redistributed *which* matchups favor rushing more than it reduced rushing's overall edge.
- **One number to verify before trusting any of the above too far:** the 45 studs/s "after" chain speed feeding this whole re-simulation is estimated from the new slide-jump caps, not measured in Play the way the original 59 was. Worth an actual Play-mode measurement before deciding the Cryo/contact-time improvements are real and not an artifact of that estimate.

**TIMING BUG FOUND IN THE 1V1 SIM ITSELF (2026-10-17, found while building the team-fight simulator below).** The owner's own `team_sim.py` write-up flags that the original 1v1 sim's 0.1s step size understated every fast-firing M1: Wind Blade by 40%, Chain Bolt by 20%, Icicle Shard by 17%. Reactions are under 2% of total damage team-wide, with one sharp exception: **W2's Overload (Pyro×Voltaic) is the single most valuable reaction pair in the dataset**, worth ~24 points via its stagger/knockback — it's most of why the "weak" comp isn't actually weak.
- **The live-Studio-values cross-check holds the conclusions** — strategy still beats stacking, same shape, slightly sharper (mono-Terra benefits further from Boulder Shot's live 200 studs/s).
- Full recommendations (10, each tested as a what-if and confidence-rated) and 12 open questions are in the report. The single most load-bearing one: **recommendation 1, allow each Element once per team** — removes the whole mono-stack/W2 exception class at once, high confidence, though the resulting 84 legal comps weren't themselves simulated yet.

**P26 — Deaths feel instant: is round-elimination (a full wipe ends the round outright) the wrong structure for team mode, separate from any number tuning?**
Status: ROOT CAUSE RESOLVED (2026-10-18), structural question still open. **Confirmed with real sim data, not just the hunch below: the HP-floor + healing-scale fix works.** The `SPELLSHOT_TEAM_MODE_PACKAGES.md` v1 package (HP 250/450/225, reworked Radiant kit, buffed wall, nerfed Fireball, etc.) takes mean round length from ~10s to 18–20s — inside the 15–30s Marvel Rivals target — and it holds with or without the "cover each other's back" teamwork AI, and in both jump variants. This validates the 2026-10-17 reordering: the two cheap number tests (HP, healing) came first and worked, so the big structural decision below may not even be necessary — rounds already have real exchange and comeback room without touching elimination format at all. **Still open:** whether 3v3 needs anything beyond number tuning given this result; owner's prior call stands either way — Duel keeps its own quick pacing, team mode is free to set its own.
- Marvel Rivals' core competitive modes (Domination, Convoy, Convergence) aren't round-elimination at all — they're ongoing objective contests with individual respawns. Dying in 5 seconds there is a blip: you respawn and rejoin the fight.
- **Checked the actual numbers, since "they just have more HP" undersells it.** [Marvel Rivals' own Duelists](https://marvelrivals.fandom.com/wiki/Heroes) — the squishy damage-dealer role, the real comparison point, not the 500–800 HP Vanguards — run 250–300 HP. That's higher than our 150 but not dramatically so.
- **Correction (2026-10-17, cont.), from the deeper roster pull — damage was never the real outlier, and the earlier framing below overstated it.** Comparing real, played-out combat averages rather than ability tooltips: SPELLSHOT's actual realized damage output (~14.6 dps, back-computed from the sim's own 10.3s 1v1 average) is *lower* than Marvel Rivals' real average (~22 dps across every role, not just Duelists) — Fireball's ~30-dps tooltip number used below was never a fair comparison, since it's rarely sustained in real play either. **The real gap is HP floor plus healing/mitigation scale.** Marvel Rivals' Strategists heal for real at ~29–31 HP/s — more than a Duelist's own real damage output — and Vanguards get real mitigation around ~43–47/s on top of their bigger HP pool. SPELLSHOT's Radiant heals at about half that real rate (15 HP/s) and most Elements get zero mitigation at all (P27). Full breakdown in `MarvelRivals_Character_Reference.md`.
- **Root cause isn't team-mode-specific — it's already present in 1v1 Duel's own numbers.** The sim report's own finding: 3v3 rounds wipe in about the same time as a 1v1 Duel (10.3s best-play) — the fast-death problem isn't created by team focus-fire, it's already there with one attacker and one target. Given the correction above, the lever isn't "cut damage" — it's raising the HP floor (150 is notably below MR's 250 squishy floor) and/or raising healing/mitigation toward a scale comparable to damage, not a small top-up. SPELLSHOT currently has mana regen (7/s) but no passive HP regen at all, and Radiant's heal running at roughly half the ratio Marvel Rivals uses. This affects Duel too, independent of P26's structure question, and overlaps with P27's survivability-tool gap.
- Open options for 3v3's structure specifically, not yet decided: (a) keep elimination but make rounds structurally longer via P27 and the regen/HP point above; (b) give team mode a more forgiving structure — in-round respawn, or an objective/score win condition; (c) a hybrid shared "life pool" per team (round ends after N total deaths, not the first wipe).
- **Tested and confirmed (2026-10-17, cont.): redistributing the existing HP budget by role does not fix round length.** Owner ran the Pass 2 role-HP hypothesis (Terra 230 / Umbra+Voltaic 120 / others 150–160) against flat 150. Round length barely moved — 10.4s vs 10.3s. What it did do: Terra's own death rate roughly halved (58–61%→36–40% of rounds) and it lives ~2.5s longer, while the 120-HP picks got hurt worse (Voltaic dies in ~90% of rounds, ~5.6s), which drags comps carrying one down even if they also carry a buffed Terra (S2: 57%→54%, a regression). Net: Role HP is doing its "give each role an identity" job, not a "make fights last longer" job — those need separate fixes. Strengthens the case for testing passive HP regen (or a flat across-the-board HP raise) as the actual round-length lever, independent of role differentiation.

**P27 — Defensive/escape tools are unevenly distributed across Elements: only Terra (wall), Radiant (heals), Cryo (CC/freeze) and Aqua (knockback peel) have one; Zephyr has mobility instead; Pyro, Voltaic, Toxin and Umbra currently have none of the above.**
Status: OPEN (2026-10-17, cont.)
- Marvel Rivals gives every role *some* way to survive focus fire, by design: Vanguards get 500–1000 HP plus shields/barriers; Strategists get heals, shields, and often enough CC to defend themselves solo (Mantis's sleep, Luna Snow's freeze, Invisible Woman's shield+invisibility); Duelists get comparatively low HP but are compensated with mobility/range and are explicitly expected to retreat rather than tank hits.
- SPELLSHOT's flat 150 HP means there's no Vanguard-style bulk-HP tank at all — Terra's wall is a tool, not a buffer, and even the hypothetical Pass 2 draft (Terra 230 HP) is a much smaller multiple than Marvel Rivals uses. More pressingly: four of nine Elements (Pyro, Voltaic, Toxin, Umbra) have neither a shield/heal/CC tool nor real escape mobility to compensate for being just as squishy as everyone else — they're glass with no cannon-specific answer, unlike Marvel Rivals' duelists, who at least get the mobility/range MR's designers traded in for their missing defense.
- Open question: is this an oversight to fix (give each of the four at least one minimal defensive or escape answer), or an intentional "all-in, no safety net" design for those four — and if intentional, does round-elimination (P26) make that design punishing rather than fun? Needs an explicit decision, not an assumption either way.

**Confirmed in the live data (2026-10-18), from the v3 package's 84-comp round-robin top/bottom-6 comp lists — this is the first direct empirical evidence for the hypothesis above, not just the role-table gap it was inferred from:**
- **Voltaic appears in 5 of the 6 worst-performing comps in v3, and none of those 5 include Terra.** It also appears twice in the top 6 — and both of those *do* include Terra (Pyro+Terra+Voltaic 86, Terra+Toxin+Voltaic 83). Voltaic isn't flatly weak; it's fine behind someone else's wall and collapses without one.
- **Umbra shows the same pattern**: 4 of 6 worst comps, zero of them with Terra.
- Aqua and Zephyr (also below breakeven at 44%/43% in v3) don't show this concentration — their weakness reads as flatter/more uniform, not tied specifically to the presence or absence of a tank. This narrows P27's fix to Voltaic and Umbra specifically as the clearest cases, rather than all four originally flagged Elements equally (Pyro is separately resolved — no longer an outlier after the damage nerf; Toxin sits near breakeven and isn't concentrated at the bottom the same way).
**TESTED AND MOSTLY REJECTED (2026-10-18), full results in `SPELLSHOT_P27_RESULTS.md`.** The role-based candidate fix (`SPELLSHOT_P27_Voltaic_Umbra_Fix_Spec.md`: Voltaic's Static Discharge, Umbra's defensive Shadow Step) was run on the 84-comp round-robin at three strengths each, independently fact-checked, with one missing control added as a result. Verdict:
- **Static Discharge (Voltaic): failed outright, don't ship.** Every variant (reactive, manual, oversized) made Voltaic *worse* (-0.7 to -1.4 points), death rate unmoved (71%→72%). Cause: the retreat pulls Voltaic out of its own Chain Bolt range, trading damage for a distance Voltaic's actual attackers — three ranged focus-fire sources, not melee divers — don't even need to close.
- **Defensive Shadow Step (Umbra): within noise, no effect.** Umbra rarely gets dove on in the first place (0.17 blink-outs/round in the baseline), so there's nothing for a vanish window to interrupt.

This folds P27's open question for Voltaic/Umbra into P28 rather than resolving it separately — see P28 for the live next step (option (b), a second Element with real team protection, e.g. Aqua's Twin Tide walls blocking shots).
- Untested follow-ups the report itself flags, not yet tried: a real dive-and-exit for Umbra needs its own separate charge (one shared cooldown can't do both); Voltaic's actual lever within its Offence role is probably damage/reach, not escape.
- **Correction (2026-10-18, from the P28/P29 round):** every sim run before this one, including this entry's own numbers, modeled Cryo's Blizzard and Deep Freeze as aimed abilities. They're actually self-centered live (see P29). This overstated Cryo by about 1.9 points throughout — doesn't change this entry's Voltaic/Umbra conclusions, but flag it if any Cryo-specific number from before 2026-10-18 gets reused.

**P28 — SPELLSHOT has exactly one tank. Once protection matters (the fix for P26/P27), every comp wants Terra, and the comp-balance spread doesn't shrink — it just relocates.**
Status: OPEN, high priority (2026-10-18), from `SPELLSHOT_TEAM_MODE_PACKAGES.md`'s 84-comp round-robin (3,486 pairings).
- **The headline number that didn't move:** the spread between the best and worst of the 84 one-per-team comps stays at ~20–21 points standard deviation in every package (current game 21, v1 21, v2 21, v3 20) — essentially unchanged despite round length, Pyro's dominance (1.6×→1.0×), and healing (6%→15–17%) all hitting their targets. **What happened instead: dominance relocated, not shrank.** Today, Pyro decides comps (71% mean win rate). The wall's own buff size and Terra's HP contribute only ~2 points each — the teamwork rule is the single biggest factor, bigger than any number in the package. Own quote from the report: "Grouping around a tank genuinely helps a tank comp, so this isn't purely a sim flaw" — i.e. this may be a real coordination dynamic, not an AI artifact, which makes it harder to dismiss.

Falls short of the package's own stated success criterion ("tank + support comps do well but don't automatically win") in a more specific way than the headline spread number shows.

- **Option (b) just got real evidence behind it (2026-10-18), not just logical appeal.** P27's self-sufficiency test (Voltaic/Umbra escape tools) came back confirming the mechanism directly from the sim: every bottom-6 comp lacks both Terra and Pyro, every top-6 comp has Terra, in every run tested. Individual survival tools don't touch that; only team-level protection does. Option (b) — a second Element with real team protection — is now the best-supported of the three options, not just the untested one left on the table.
